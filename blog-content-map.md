## PART 2 — KEYWORD & CANNIBALIZATION STRATEGY

### Highest-priority topics
The commercial-investigation and comparison pieces that support MiNa's thinnest or
highest-value service pages carry the most weight: implants (#27–33), cosmetic
decision guides (#15–17), Invisalign candidacy (#23–24), periodontics early-signs
(#34–37), and the local family-dentist guide (#1). These align off-site informational
demand with on-site commercial pages.

### Keywords we deliberately do NOT target aggressively
We avoid stacking exact-match commercial head terms — "best dentist in Thornhill",
"dentist in Thornhill", "Thornhill dentist" — across many articles. Only #1 and #4
carry light local-commercial intent; the rest stay informational. This prevents the
portfolio from reading as a doorway network and keeps it from cannibalizing MiNa's own
homepage and service pages, which should own those commercial terms directly.

### How the articles support existing MiNa service pages
Each off-site article answers an *informational* question and links to the matching
*commercial* MiNa page, passing topical relevance without duplicating that page's
purpose. Example: #9 (deep cleaning vs regular cleaning — informational) supports
`/services/periodontics/` without competing to rank for "periodontist Thornhill."

### Topical-authority logic
The 50 pieces form nine semantic clusters covering MiNa's full service taxonomy
(prevention, cosmetic, ortho, implants, perio, endo/surgery/emergency, pediatric,
senior, and Canadian funding). Dense internal linking within clusters, plus
cross-cluster "Related Reading," builds an entity graph around the MiNa dentists and
their service areas.

### Anchor-text distribution
No exact-match commercial anchor is repeated. Anchors skew descriptive and varied —
"a local family dental team", "scaling and root planing", "the dental implant process",
"a periodontist", "cosmetic options for adults". Roughly 70% descriptive-topical,
20% branded/entity, 10% navigational. Each article carries **one** primary contextual
link; a second only where genuinely useful.

### Geographic distribution
Thornhill / Yonge Street / GTA references appear naturally in Cluster A (local) and
lightly in Clusters H and I. Clinical clusters (B–G) stay largely location-neutral —
a dental-education reader doesn't need a city name in an article about how tartar forms.
This keeps local signals concentrated where they're credible rather than smeared across
every page.
