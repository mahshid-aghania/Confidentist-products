The Canadian Dental Care Plan, Explained for Ontario Patients

The CDCP has changed how many Canadians without private insurance pay for dental care. Here's a plain overview of what it is, who qualifies, and how it works in practice.

ConfiDentist Editorial Team • 8 min read

The Canadian Dental Care Plan (CDCP) is a federal program, administered through Health Canada, that helps cover the cost of dental care for eligible Canadian residents who do not have access to private dental insurance. If cost has kept dental visits off your calendar, it's worth understanding how the plan is meant to work.

A quick note before we go further: the CDCP's details — covered services, co-payment bands, enrolment dates — are set by the Government of Canada and can change as the program matures. This article explains the general shape of the plan. For the rules that apply to you right now, always check the official Government of Canada page.

What the CDCP is meant to do

The plan exists to reduce a long-standing gap: many Canadians, particularly seniors, lower-income households and people between jobs, have had no dental coverage at all. The CDCP is designed to help these residents afford routine and necessary dental care rather than putting it off until a small problem becomes an emergency.

Crucially, it is aimed at people without private insurance. It isn't a top-up to an existing employer or personal plan — more on that distinction below.

For context, dental care sits outside Canada's publicly funded medical system in most situations. Unlike a visit to a family doctor, routine dental treatment has historically been paid for out of pocket or through private insurance, which left many residents without reliable coverage. The CDCP is the federal response to that gap, and it represents one of the larger expansions of dental coverage the country has seen.

Who is eligible

Eligibility rests on a few core conditions that have stayed consistent as the program rolled out:

  • No access to private dental insurance. This is the central requirement. If you can get dental coverage through an employer, a pension, a spouse's plan or a plan you've bought yourself, you generally aren't eligible — even if you choose not to use it.
  • Adjusted family net income under $90,000. The plan is income-tested, with the threshold based on adjusted family net income.
  • Filed a tax return. Because eligibility is assessed from income, you (and your spouse or common-law partner, if applicable) need to have filed a return for the relevant year.
  • Canadian residency. You must be a resident of Canada for tax purposes.

Those are the pillars. The precise wording, any additional conditions, and how income is calculated are detailed on the official site.

Check the Source

Because the CDCP is still evolving, the single most reliable step you can take is to read the current rules directly from the Government of Canada. Eligibility details, application steps and coverage specifics are kept up to date there.

How the rollout has worked

The CDCP didn't open to everyone at once. It was introduced in phases, with different age groups and categories becoming eligible to apply over time — seniors in the earlier waves, with eligibility broadening to additional groups as the program expanded. If you weren't eligible to apply when you first heard about the plan, it's worth checking again, because the eligible population has widened.

This phased approach is one reason there's been some public confusion about the plan. People who applied early, people invited to apply by letter, and people who became eligible later have all had slightly different experiences, and timelines have shifted as the program scaled up. Rather than relying on what a neighbour went through a year ago, the safer move is to look at where the rollout stands today on the official page and apply based on the current process.

A note on applying

Application details — how to apply, what information you'll need, and when coverage begins after approval — are handled by the Government of Canada and have evolved as the program has grown. Because this is exactly the kind of detail that changes, this article won't spell out a step-by-step application process that might be out of date by the time you read it. The official page walks through the current steps clearly.

The co-payment concept

The CDCP is not identical to "free dentistry for everyone who qualifies". It uses an income-based co-payment. In general terms:

  • Households at the lower end of the income range typically pay no co-payment for covered services.
  • Households in the higher income bands within the eligibility range pay a share of the cost, with the plan covering the rest.

There's an important nuance: the plan reimburses covered services at established rates, and a provider's fee may differ from that amount. That means you could have a cost beyond the co-payment in some situations. The official page explains how this works, and a dental office can walk you through what to expect before treatment begins.

Using the plan at a dental office

Once you've applied and been approved, you receive confirmation of your coverage and the date it begins. From there, the practical steps are straightforward:

  1. Confirm your office participates. Dental offices choose whether to take part in the plan. When booking, simply ask whether they accept the CDCP.
  2. Bring your coverage information. Share your plan details so the office can bill covered services correctly.
  3. Ask about any co-payment or additional cost up front. A clear estimate before treatment avoids surprises.

It's also sensible to go in with realistic expectations about coverage. The CDCP reimburses a defined set of services, and some treatments or materials may fall partly or wholly outside what the plan pays. That doesn't mean you can't choose them — it just means part of the cost could be yours. A short conversation with the office before treatment, paired with the coverage detail on the official page, is the best way to avoid surprises.

Many offices across Ontario, including in the Thornhill and greater Toronto area, participate in the plan. If you'd like to make an appointment under the CDCP, it helps to confirm participation first with a dental office that accepts the CDCP so billing is handled smoothly from the start.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who is eligible for the Canadian Dental Care Plan?

Eligibility centres on being a Canadian resident without access to private dental insurance, an adjusted family net income under $90,000, and having filed a tax return for the relevant year. Full criteria are on the Government of Canada site.

Do I pay anything if I'm on the CDCP?

It depends on income. Lower-income households generally pay no co-payment; higher income bands within the eligibility range pay a share. The official page explains the current co-payment structure and how reimbursement rates work.

How do I use the CDCP at a dental office?

Once approved, you receive your coverage details and confirm that your dental office participates. The office bills covered services accordingly, and you pay any co-payment that applies. Confirm participation when you book.
