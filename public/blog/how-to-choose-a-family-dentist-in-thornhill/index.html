How to Choose a Family Dentist in Thornhill: 7 Questions Worth Asking

Picking a dental home for your whole household is a decision you'll live with for years. A short, focused conversation before you book can tell you most of what you need to know.

Reviewed by Dr. Neda Kadivar, General & Family Dentist • 8 min read

Most people choose a dentist the way they choose a plumber — whoever is closest and takes their insurance. That works until a filling cracks on a Friday night, or until you're trying to book your toddler and your father-in-law into the same practice. A little homework up front saves a lot of switching later.

The good news is that you don't need to be a clinician to ask good questions. You just need to know which ones separate a clinic that will genuinely fit your family from one that happens to be nearby. Here are seven.

1. Does the practice actually treat every age under one roof?

"Family dentist" is a description, not a protected title. Some practices that use the term are most comfortable with adults and refer children out; others see toddlers but send implant or gum cases elsewhere. Ask directly: do you see children, and do you handle more involved treatment in-house or by referral?

A practice that offers general, preventive, cosmetic, restorative and surgical care in one place means fewer handoffs — which matters most when a family member needs something beyond a routine cleaning.

2. How are emergencies handled?

Dental emergencies rarely happen during business hours. Ask how the clinic responds to a broken tooth, lost crown or sudden swelling: Do they reserve same-day slots? Is there an after-hours number? Will they see you if you're a new patient in pain?

The answer tells you how the practice prioritizes existing patients when something goes wrong — which is exactly when you'll care most.

3. Where is it, really — and when is it open?

Convenience is underrated. A clinic you can reach on the way home, with evening or weekend hours, is one you'll actually keep appointments at. For households near the Yonge Street corridor, proximity to transit and parking makes the difference between a standing six-month habit and a lapsed one.

Ask about the realistic wait for a routine cleaning, too. A practice that can't fit you in for three months isn't convenient no matter how close it is.

4. How does the clinic explain treatment and cost?

You want a dentist who shows you what they see — on the X-ray or intraoral photo — and explains why a treatment is recommended, including the option of monitoring rather than treating right away. A clear written estimate before work begins is a reasonable expectation, not a special request.

Worth Knowing

Since 2024, the Canadian Dental Care Plan (CDCP) has expanded coverage to eligible residents without private insurance. If cost is a factor for your family, ask whether a clinic accepts the CDCP before you book.

5. What does the first visit include?

A thorough new-patient appointment usually goes beyond a quick look. Expect a review of your medical history, an oral cancer screening, a gum (periodontal) assessment, and X-rays if you don't have recent ones to transfer. If a clinic rushes straight to cleaning without any of this, that's worth noting.

6. Who will you actually see?

In some offices you see a different provider each visit; in others you build a relationship with one dentist and hygienist over years. Neither is wrong, but continuity matters for families managing ongoing issues — orthodontic monitoring in a teenager, gum health in an adult, denture adjustments in a grandparent. Ask what to expect.

7. How does the clinic approach prevention?

The best dental care is the care you end up not needing. A practice oriented toward prevention will talk about home habits, recall intervals tailored to your risk, and early intervention — not just treatment after problems appear. That mindset is especially valuable when children are forming lifelong habits.

Putting it together

You won't get through all seven questions in a single phone call, and you don't need to. Use the first two or three to screen your shortlist, then judge the rest at your first appointment. A clinic that answers openly, doesn't rush you, and treats your questions as reasonable is usually one worth staying with.

In Thornhill, families near the Yonge and Steeles area have several options within a short drive. A practice such as a local family dental team that cares for children, adults and seniors in one location can simplify scheduling across a multi-generation household — but the right fit is the one whose answers to the questions above line up with what your family actually needs.

Frequently Asked Questions

At what age should a child first see a family dentist?

The Canadian Dental Association recommends a first visit within six months of the first tooth appearing, or by one year of age — whichever comes first. Early visits are short and focus on getting a child comfortable.

Is a "family dentist" different from a "general dentist"?

Not really. A family dentist is a general dentist who treats patients of all ages. The label simply signals that the practice is set up to care for children, adults and seniors together.

How do I move my records to a new clinic?

Ask your new office to request records and recent X-rays from your previous dentist. With a signed consent form, most transfers are handled office-to-office so you don't repeat imaging unnecessarily.
