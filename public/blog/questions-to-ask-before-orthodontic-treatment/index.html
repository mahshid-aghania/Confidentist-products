Clear Aligners & Orthodontics

Questions Worth Asking Before Starting Any Orthodontic Treatment

Straightening your teeth is a months-long commitment of time and money. A handful of clear questions up front tells you whether a plan is complete — and whether the person presenting it is being straight with you.

Reviewed by Dr. Neda Kadivar, General & Family Dentist • 7 min read

Before you agree to braces, aligners or any plan to move your teeth, it pays to ask the same kinds of questions you would about any significant commitment: who is doing the work, what the plan is based on, what it really costs, and what happens afterward. Good providers welcome these questions. Here is a checklist to take with you.

Whether you are sitting with a general dentist, a specialist or a direct-to-consumer brand, the questions below cut through the marketing and get at what matters. You don't need to ask all of them in order — think of it as a set to pull from.

Who is actually providing my care?

Find out who will plan and supervise your treatment, and what their experience is with cases like yours. Will a dentist examine you in person and monitor progress, or is the plan driven by a remote model with little hands-on oversight? Orthodontics is a clinical process, not a product you assemble at home — knowing there is an accountable professional watching your teeth move is foundational.

If the person planning your care is a general dentist rather than a specialist, that is not a problem in itself — many general dentists treat orthodontic cases well, particularly straightforward to moderate ones. The point of asking is simply to understand their experience with cases like yours and when they would refer to a specialist. A provider who is candid about the kinds of cases they take on, and the ones they hand off, is one who knows their own limits.

What is the plan based on?

Ask what diagnostics inform the plan. A thorough assessment usually includes an examination of your teeth and gums, a review of your dental and medical history, and imaging or a digital scan. Be cautious of any plan that proposes moving teeth without first checking the health of the gums and bone that hold them. The quality of the diagnosis shapes the quality of the result.

What does it cost — and what's included?

Get the total, not a teaser. A complete quote should spell out whether it covers records and imaging, all adjustment or check-in visits, retainers at the end, and any refinements. The most common surprise is discovering that retainers or a round of refinements are billed separately. Ask plainly: "Is there anything I'll be asked to pay for later that isn't in this number?"

Worth Knowing

Retention is not an optional add-on. Because teeth tend to drift back after treatment, retainers are a permanent part of keeping your result. If a plan or quote doesn't mention them at all, that is a gap worth raising before you commit.

Be wary, too, of a quote that exists only to secure a deposit before you have the full picture. A reasonable practice will give you time to understand the plan, ask follow-ups, and compare — not pressure you to commit on the spot with a limited-time discount. Orthodontic treatment is a long relationship, and the tone of the sales conversation often previews the tone of the care.

How long will it take, realistically?

Ask for an honest estimated timeline and what it depends on. Treatment length varies with the complexity of your case and, for removable appliances, how consistently you wear them. A trustworthy answer includes the caveats — "if everything goes to plan, roughly this long, and here's what could extend it" — rather than a confident single number that ignores biology.

What about retainers and keeping the result?

Beyond confirming retainers are included, ask what kind, how long you will wear them, and what the long-term routine looks like (for many people, nightly, indefinitely). Understanding the retention commitment now prevents the all-too-common story of a straightened smile relapsing because retainers quietly fell out of the routine.

What happens if teeth don't finish as planned — or relapse later?

Two related questions. First, if your teeth haven't landed exactly where intended at the end, are refinements available and how are they handled? Second, if teeth drift after treatment, what is the provider's approach — and is it covered, or a fresh cost? These scenarios are not rare, and a provider who has clear, calm answers is one who has thought past the sale.

What happens if something goes wrong mid-treatment?

Treatment rarely runs for a year or more without a hiccup. A tray cracks, an aligner is lost, a bracket comes loose, or a tooth isn't tracking the way the plan expected. Ask how the practice handles these day-to-day realities: how quickly you can be seen, whether replacement trays carry a cost, and how progress is checked so a tooth that is falling behind is caught early rather than at the finish line.

It is also fair to ask what support looks like between visits — who you contact if something feels wrong, and how responsive the practice tends to be. A plan that only accounts for the ideal path isn't really a complete plan.

Does my overall dental health come first?

One question that is easy to overlook: is my mouth healthy enough to start? Moving teeth assumes the gums and bone supporting them are in good shape. Ask whether any decay, gum inflammation or other issues should be treated before orthodontics begins. A provider who insists on a healthy foundation first is protecting your result, not stalling it — rushing teeth through unhealthy tissue can do real harm.

Putting the checklist to work

You will learn as much from how these questions are answered as from the answers themselves. A provider who is specific, unhurried and comfortable discussing limits and trade-offs is giving you a good sign. One who deflects, over-promises, or treats reasonable questions as an inconvenience is giving you a different kind of signal.

Remember too that no honest professional can promise an exact outcome before examining you — results and candidacy depend on your individual clinical situation. If you would like a consultation where these questions are genuinely welcome, you can arrange an orthodontic consultation and bring this checklist along.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should an orthodontic quote include?

A clear quote should cover the full treatment, not just the trays or brackets — records and imaging, all adjustment or monitoring visits, retainers, and any planned refinements. Ask specifically what is and isn't included so a later cost isn't a surprise.

Why do I need retainers after treatment?

Teeth naturally tend to drift back toward their former positions once they have been moved. Retainers hold the result, and most people wear them long-term, often nightly, to prevent relapse. A retention plan is part of any complete treatment.

What are refinements?

Refinements are extra trays or adjustments added near the end of treatment to fine-tune the result if teeth haven't landed exactly as planned. Ask in advance whether they are included and how they are handled, since plans differ.
