Switching Dentists Without the Hassle: A Step-by-Step Guide

Most people stay with a dentist they've outgrown simply because changing feels like a chore. It isn't. Here's the whole process, start to finish.

Reviewed by Dr. Neda Kadivar, General & Family Dentist • 7 min read

Switching dentists is nowhere near as complicated as the inertia around it suggests. You don't need permission, you don't lose your coverage, and in most cases you don't even have to call your old office — the new one can handle the paperwork. The real work is just deciding to do it and lining the steps up in the right order.

If you've been meaning to make a change but weren't sure where to start, here's a clean walk-through.

Step 1: Decide whether you're switching for the right reason

Not every frustration warrants a move, and not every reason to move is obvious. It's worth sorting the two before you go to the trouble.

Reasons that usually justify a switch

  • You've moved, and the commute no longer makes sense.
  • The office can't offer hours or scheduling that fit your life.
  • You never feel your questions are welcome, or treatment is never explained.
  • You want one practice that can treat your whole family under one roof.

Reasons that might be worth a conversation first

  • A single bad appointment that may not reflect the usual standard.
  • A cost surprise that a clearer estimate process could have prevented.

Step 2: Choose and vet the new office before you leave

Line up your destination first, so there's no gap in your care. Confirm the basics: do they treat the ages you need, how do they handle emergencies, what are the hours, and — crucially — do they accept your insurance or the Canadian Dental Care Plan? Sorting coverage up front avoids the most common post-switch headache.

Worth Knowing

Your dental records belong to you in the sense that you're entitled to have them transferred. A clinic keeps the original file, but it must release copies — including recent X-rays — to another dentist with your written consent. You don't need to justify the request.

Step 3: Transfer your records and X-rays

This is the part people dread needlessly. In practice, you sign a consent form at your new office and they request your records and recent radiographs from the previous dentist on your behalf. The transfer happens clinic-to-clinic. Having your imaging move with you means you usually avoid repeating X-rays — saving time, and unnecessary exposure.

If you'd rather manage it yourself, you can contact the old office directly and ask them to forward a copy of your chart and images to the new one. Either path works.

Step 4: Time the move around your recall

The smoothest moment to switch is right before your next check-up is due. That way your first appointment at the new clinic doubles as your routine visit, and there's no awkward overlap or lapse. If you're mid-treatment — say, partway through a crown or a root canal — it's generally wiser to finish that course of care before transferring, unless there's a pressing reason not to.

Step 5: Keep coverage continuity in mind

Your insurance or CDCP eligibility follows you, not the clinic, so switching doesn't reset anything. What can trip people up is annual maximums and whether the new office bills your plan directly. Ask how they handle claims on your first call. For CDCP specifically, the only thing that changes is which participating provider you see — confirm the new clinic accepts it.

Red flags worth noticing on the way out — or in

As you compare, a few signals are worth weighing. An office that resists releasing your records, pressures you toward large treatment plans without clear explanation, or can never give a written estimate is telling you something. On the other side, a new clinic that welcomes your questions, explains the first visit clearly, and makes transferring easy is usually a good sign.

Putting it in motion

Once you've picked a destination, the fastest way to start is simply to request a first appointment and let the new team guide the records transfer. If you're ready to take that step, learn what's involved in booking a first visit as a new patient and go from there.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I have to tell my old dentist I'm leaving?

No — you don't owe anyone an explanation. The simplest route is to let your new office request your records and X-rays directly with your signed consent, so you may not need to contact the old clinic at all.

Will I lose my insurance or CDCP coverage if I switch?

No. Coverage follows you, not the clinic. Just confirm the new office accepts your plan — or the Canadian Dental Care Plan, if that applies — before you book.

Will I have to repeat X-rays at a new office?

Usually not, provided your recent images are transferred. New X-rays are taken only when the existing ones are too old or don't show what the dentist needs.
