Teeth Whitening Options Compared: In-Office, Take-Home, and Over-the-Counter

The three routes to a brighter smile differ in more than price. Concentration, supervision and the kind of stain you have all shape what you'll actually see.

Reviewed by Dr. Neda Kadivar, General & Family Dentist • 7 min read

Teeth whitening comes down to a single chemistry: a peroxide gel breaks down coloured molecules inside the enamel. Every method on the market uses some version of it. What separates them is the strength of the gel, whether a professional supervises its use, and how carefully your teeth and gums are protected while it works. Those three variables explain most of the difference you'll notice.

Broadly there are three routes — in-office whitening at a dental clinic, dentist-prescribed take-home trays, and over-the-counter strips and toothpastes from a pharmacy. Each suits a different mix of budget, timeline and sensitivity, and none of them changes the colour of every surface in your mouth. Here is how they compare.

In-office (professional) whitening

Performed at the clinic, in-office whitening uses the highest peroxide concentrations because the gums and soft tissue are isolated and protected first, often with a light or lamp to speed the reaction. It is the fastest route — a single appointment can produce a visible change — and it happens under direct supervision, which matters if you have sensitive teeth or existing restorations the dentist needs to account for.

The trade-off is cost: a chairside session typically sits at the higher end of the whitening options. Some people also notice short-lived sensitivity afterward, which usually settles within a day or two.

Dentist-prescribed take-home trays

Here the dentist takes impressions and makes custom trays that fit your teeth closely, then provides a professional-strength gel to use at home over a stretch of days or weeks. The concentration is lower than an in-office session but higher than anything sold over the counter, and the custom fit keeps the gel on the teeth rather than irritating the gums.

Take-home whitening builds results more gradually and gives you control over the pace, which can be gentler for sensitive teeth. Because the trays are reusable, it is also a practical way to top up colour later. It costs less than in-office whitening but more than drugstore products.

Over-the-counter strips and toothpastes

Pharmacy strips, pens and whitening toothpastes are the most accessible and least expensive option. They are deliberately formulated at low peroxide concentrations for unsupervised use, so they are safer to use without professional oversight but work more slowly and with more modest results. Strips rely on a one-size shape that may not reach every tooth evenly, and whitening toothpastes mostly remove surface stains rather than lightening the enamel itself.

Why some stains barely budge

Whitening is reliable on extrinsic staining — the yellowing from coffee, tea, red wine, tobacco and ordinary ageing that sits in and on the enamel. It is far less predictable on intrinsic discolouration that comes from inside the tooth: staining from certain antibiotics taken in childhood, from trauma to a tooth, or from excess fluoride during development. Those cases sometimes need a different cosmetic approach, such as bonding or veneers, rather than more peroxide.

Worth Knowing

Whitening never changes the colour of fillings, crowns, bonding or veneers. If you have restorations on your front teeth, whitening can leave them looking darker than the surrounding enamel — a reason to plan the order of any cosmetic work with your dentist.

How long results last

No whitening result is permanent. Teeth gradually pick up colour again from food, drink and age, so most people see some fading over months to a couple of years depending on their habits. Take-home trays make touch-ups straightforward; in-office results can be maintained with occasional at-home top-ups. Cutting back on staining drinks and not smoking does more to protect the result than any single product.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does professional whitening work faster than store-bought products?

Professional gels use higher peroxide concentrations than products sold over the counter, applied under supervision with the gums protected. Drugstore products are deliberately milder for unsupervised use, so they work more gradually.

Why didn't whitening change the colour of some of my teeth?

Whitening only lightens natural enamel. It does not affect fillings, crowns, bonding or veneers, and some intrinsic stains — from certain medications or past trauma — respond poorly to peroxide.

Does whitening damage enamel or cause lasting sensitivity?

Sensitivity during or just after whitening is common and usually temporary. A dentist can lower the risk by assessing your enamel first and adjusting the concentration or timing, which is part of why supervised whitening is often preferred for sensitive teeth.
