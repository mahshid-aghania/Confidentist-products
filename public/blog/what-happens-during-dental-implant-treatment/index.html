Dental Implants

What Happens During Dental Implant Treatment, Step by Step

Implant treatment feels less daunting once you can see the whole arc of it — from the first scan, through a quiet healing stretch, to the day the crown goes on.

Reviewed by Dr. Mehdi Adibrad, Dental Surgeon • 8 min read

Dental implant treatment is a staged process, not a single appointment. In broad terms it runs through four phases: a planning consultation with imaging, the surgical placement of the implant, a healing period while bone bonds to it, and finally the abutment and crown that complete the tooth. Knowing what each phase involves — and roughly how long it takes — removes most of the uncertainty.

The timeline below is a general map. The actual schedule depends on the site in the mouth, the condition of the bone, your general health, and whether any groundwork such as grafting is needed first. Your dentist builds the real plan around your examination.

Step 1 — Consultation and imaging

The first visit is about information, not treatment. The dentist reviews your medical history and medications, examines the gap and surrounding teeth, and checks gum health. Imaging is central here: alongside standard X-rays, a cone-beam CT (CBCT) scan is often taken. This 3D scan shows the height, width and density of bone, and the position of nerves and sinuses — all of which determine where and whether an implant can go.

From this, a plan is made. If there isn't enough bone, grafting or a sinus lift may be recommended before placement, which adds a healing stage of its own. If the site is ready, the placement can be scheduled.

Step 2 — Placement surgery

Placing the implant is a minor surgical procedure, usually done under local anaesthetic so the area is fully numb; sedation options may be available for anxious patients. The dentist makes a small opening in the gum, prepares a precise channel in the bone, and positions the titanium fixture into it. The gum is then closed with stitches around or over the implant.

The appointment itself is typically shorter than people expect for a single implant. A temporary tooth or cover may be fitted depending on the case and location.

What to Expect Afterward

Mild swelling, some bruising and tenderness for a few days are normal and usually managed with over-the-counter pain relief and ice. Soft foods and gentle cleaning around the site help. Any heavy bleeding, spreading swelling or fever should prompt a call to your dental office.

Step 3 — Healing and osseointegration

This is the longest and quietest phase, and the one that can't be hurried. Over several weeks to a few months, bone grows against the fixture and locks onto it — the process called osseointegration. The implant can't safely carry a crown until this bond is strong enough to take chewing forces.

Most of this period is simply living normally while the body does the work. You'll usually have a check to confirm the implant is integrating well before moving on. How long it takes varies between individuals and sites, which is why no single healing time applies to everyone.

Step 4 — Abutment and crown

Once the implant is stable, the abutment — the connector that rises through the gum — is fitted, sometimes in a brief procedure to expose the implant. The gum is given a short time to shape around it. Then an impression or digital scan is taken, and a crown is custom-made to match your neighbouring teeth in shape and shade.

At the final visit the crown is attached to the abutment, your bite is checked and adjusted, and the tooth is ready to use. For multiple-tooth cases the same principle applies, with a bridge or denture attaching to several implants.

Recovery and the bigger picture

Day-to-day recovery after each surgical step is usually straightforward, but the overall journey asks for patience — the value of an implant comes from letting the bone bond properly rather than rushing the result. Throughout, good home care and attending review appointments protect the investment.

It's also worth remembering that not everyone is a candidate, and the plan can shift as healing is assessed. The staged approach exists precisely so each step can be confirmed before the next — a feature of the process, not a delay.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does the whole process take?

From placement to final crown it commonly spans a few months, because bone needs time to bond to the implant. Timelines vary widely with the site, bone condition and whether grafting is needed, so your dentist will give a plan specific to your case.

Is implant placement painful?

Placement is done under local anaesthetic, so the area is numb during surgery. Most people manage afterward with over-the-counter pain relief and find recovery milder than expected, though experiences differ.

Why is there a waiting period before the crown?

The implant must osseointegrate — bond with the surrounding bone — before it can safely carry a crown and chewing forces. The healing period is a protective part of the plan, not an avoidable delay.
