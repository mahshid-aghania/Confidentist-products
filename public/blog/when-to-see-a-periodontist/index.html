Gum Health & Periodontics

When Should You See a Periodontist?

Most gum care happens with a general dentist. But certain situations call for a specialist in the gums and the structures that support your teeth — here is when that referral tends to come up.

Reviewed by Dr. Mehdi Adibrad, Dental Surgeon • 7 min read

A periodontist is a dentist with additional training in the gums and the bone and tissue that hold teeth in place — and in placing dental implants. You generally see one when a gum-related concern goes beyond routine care: deep pockets around teeth, signs of bone loss, noticeable recession, planning for implants, or inflammation that will not settle. Knowing the triggers helps you recognise when a specialist visit makes sense.

What a periodontist does

Think of the periodontist as the dental professional who focuses on the foundation. While a general dentist cares for the teeth across a wide range of needs, a periodontist concentrates on the supporting cast — the gums, the periodontal ligament and the bone around each tooth. Their training equips them to diagnose and treat gum disease at every stage, to perform procedures such as gum grafting and pocket reduction, and to place and maintain dental implants.

Many people never need to see one, because general dentists manage the majority of everyday gum issues. A referral usually means a particular situation would benefit from that added depth of focus.

Signs that may prompt a referral

Deep pockets around the teeth

When a dentist or hygienist measures the small spaces between gum and tooth, deeper-than-normal readings suggest that attachment has been lost and bacteria are collecting below the gum line. Persistently deep pockets are one of the clearest reasons a general dentist refers to a periodontist for more specialised management.

Signs of bone loss

Gum disease that has progressed can affect the bone supporting the teeth, and this often shows up on dental X-rays. Where bone loss is evident, a periodontist can assess its extent and advise on how to halt the process and stabilise what remains.

Noticeable gum recession

Gums that have receded — leaving teeth looking longer and roots exposed — may warrant specialist input, particularly if the recession is advancing, causing sensitivity, or affecting the firm band of gum tissue. A periodontist can determine whether a grafting procedure is appropriate.

Planning for dental implants

Because implants rely on healthy bone and gum tissue to succeed, periodontists are often involved in implant planning and placement. If you are considering an implant, a periodontal evaluation of the site is a common and sensible step.

Inflammation that will not resolve

Gums that stay red, swollen or prone to bleeding despite good home care and regular cleanings may need a closer look. Persistent inflammation can signal a process that routine care is not keeping up with, and a specialist assessment can clarify what is going on.

Worth Knowing

A referral to a periodontist is not a verdict. It usually means your dentist wants a specialist's read on a particular aspect of your gum health — much as a physician might refer you to a specialist for a focused opinion. Many referrals lead to straightforward, non-surgical care.

What the first visit usually involves

A first periodontal appointment is largely about information-gathering. You can generally expect a review of your medical and dental history, including any medications and health conditions that bear on gum health. The periodontist will examine your gums and teeth, and measure the pockets around each tooth to map where attachment has been lost. X-rays — either new ones or those forwarded from your dentist — help reveal the bone level that cannot be seen directly.

From there, the periodontist explains what they have found and discusses options. The emphasis is usually on the least invasive approach suitable for the situation, which for many people means non-surgical care such as a deeper cleaning below the gum line followed by regular maintenance. Where more is needed, they will walk you through it. A good first visit leaves you understanding your condition and the reasoning behind any recommendation.

The takeaway

You do not need to monitor your own gums closely enough to decide when a specialist is required — that is part of what regular check-ups are for. But recognising the common triggers helps the process make sense if it comes up, and makes it easier to ask good questions. If your dentist raises the idea, or if you have ongoing concerns about your gums, a consultation with a periodontist is a reasonable step toward understanding where your gum health stands.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a dentist and a periodontist?

A periodontist is a dentist who has completed additional training focused on the gums and the bone and tissue that support the teeth, as well as the placement of dental implants. A general dentist handles a broad range of care and often manages milder gum issues, referring more involved or advanced cases to a periodontist.

Do I need a referral to see a periodontist?

Often a general dentist will refer you when they identify a concern that would benefit from specialist care, but a referral is not always required to make an appointment. If you have specific concerns about your gums, it is reasonable to ask your dentist whether a periodontal consultation would be worthwhile.

Does seeing a periodontist mean I will need surgery?

Not necessarily. A periodontist evaluates the condition and recommends the least invasive approach appropriate for it. Many situations are managed without surgery, through deeper cleaning and maintenance. Surgery is one tool among several and is considered only when it is the right fit, based on an individual examination.
