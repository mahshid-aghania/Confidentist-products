AFK Preparation

How to Start Preparing for the AFK Exam

November 10, 2026ConfiDentist Team6 min read

The Assessment of Fundamental Knowledge (AFK) is the first examination on the NDEB Equivalency Process, and for many internationally trained dentists it is the gateway to practising in Canada. Starting well matters — a clear plan in your first few weeks saves months of wasted effort later. Here is how to begin.

1. Understand What the AFK Actually Tests

The AFK is a written, multiple-choice examination covering the foundational biomedical and clinical sciences expected of a graduating dentist in Canada. Before you open a single textbook, read the official NDEB exam specifications so you know exactly which domains are weighted most heavily. Preparing to the blueprint — rather than to whatever resource you happen to have — is the single biggest efficiency gain available to you.

2. Take an Honest Baseline

Sit a timed set of practice questions in week one, before studying. It will feel uncomfortable, and that is the point: you need to know where you stand. A baseline tells you which subjects need deep work and which only need review, so you can spend your limited time where it changes your score the most.

A baseline is a diagnostic, not a verdict. Almost everyone scores low at the start — what matters is the trajectory from here, not the first number.

3. Build a Realistic Weekly Schedule

Consistency beats intensity. A sustainable plan built around your actual life will carry you further than an ambitious one you abandon in three weeks. When you map out your week:

  • Block fixed study sessions in your calendar like appointments you cannot miss.
  • Rotate subjects rather than cramming one for days — spaced exposure improves retention.
  • End every session with active recall: close the book and write down what you remember.
  • Protect one lighter day a week to prevent burnout.

4. Choose a Small Set of Trusted Resources

Resource overload is one of the most common early mistakes. Pick a core reference for each subject, a solid question bank, and a concise set of notes — and resist the temptation to collect more. Depth with a few trusted resources beats shallow coverage across many.

5. Practise Under Exam Conditions Early

The AFK is as much a test of pacing and stamina as of knowledge. Start doing timed blocks early so exam-day conditions feel familiar rather than threatening. Review every question you get wrong and every one you guessed correctly — both reveal gaps worth closing.

6. Don't Study Alone the Whole Way

Structured guidance keeps you accountable and corrects misunderstandings before they harden into habits. Whether that is a study group, a mentor, or a full preparation program, having someone to check your progress makes a measurable difference — especially in the final stretch.
