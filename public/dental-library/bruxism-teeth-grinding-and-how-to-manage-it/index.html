Bruxism: Teeth Grinding and How to Manage It

Common Concerns & Symptoms4 min read

Bruxism is the clinical term for clenching or grinding your teeth, and it often happens without you realizing it, particularly during sleep. Occasional grinding may not cause harm, but when it becomes frequent or forceful it can wear down teeth and strain the jaw. The good news is that bruxism is very manageable once it is identified.

Recognizing the Signs

Because much of the grinding happens at night, people sometimes learn about it from a partner or during a routine dental exam. A few clues are worth noticing.

  • Worn, flattened, or chipped teeth
  • Increased tooth sensitivity
  • Jaw soreness or tired facial muscles in the morning
  • Dull headaches starting near the temples
  • Disrupted sleep for you or your partner

Why It Happens

Grinding is often linked to stress and anxiety, but it can also relate to sleep patterns, certain medications, or an uneven bite. Because the triggers differ from person to person, a tailored approach works best. Our dentists can examine your teeth for telltale wear and talk through possible causes; you can find out more about the services offered at our dental centre.

Practical Ways to Manage Bruxism

Treatment focuses on protecting your teeth and easing the underlying tension. A combination of measures is often most effective.

  • A custom-fitted night guard to cushion the teeth
  • Stress-management habits such as exercise or mindfulness
  • Limiting caffeine and alcohol, especially in the evening
  • Being mindful of daytime clenching and keeping teeth slightly apart

Addressing bruxism early helps preserve your natural teeth and reduces wear on existing dental work. If grinding has already caused damage, your dentist can discuss gentle options to restore and protect those teeth.
