Canker Sores and Mouth Ulcers

Common Concerns & Symptoms4 min read

Few things interrupt an ordinary day like the sting of a canker sore when you eat or talk. These small ulcers inside the mouth are very common and, while uncomfortable, are usually harmless and heal on their own. Knowing what triggers them and how to find relief can make these episodes much easier to handle.

What Canker Sores Are

Canker sores are small, shallow ulcers that appear on the soft tissues inside the mouth, such as the inner cheeks, lips, or tongue. They typically look white or yellow with a red border. Unlike cold sores, which appear on the lips and are caused by a virus, canker sores are not contagious and stay inside the mouth. Most heal within one to two weeks without treatment.

Common Triggers

The exact cause is not always clear, but several factors are often linked to canker sores.

  • Minor injuries from biting your cheek, braces, or vigorous brushing.
  • Stress or a lack of sleep.
  • Acidic or spicy foods.
  • Hormonal changes.
  • Certain nutritional gaps, such as low iron, folate, or vitamin B12.

Finding Relief and When to Seek Care

While you wait for a sore to heal, rinsing with warm salt water, avoiding spicy or acidic foods, and using over-the-counter numbing gels can ease discomfort. Keeping up gentle oral hygiene also supports healing. Most sores resolve on their own, but it is wise to check in with a dental professional if a sore lasts longer than two weeks, is unusually large or painful, or keeps returning, so any underlying cause can be addressed.
