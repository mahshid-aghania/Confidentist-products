Caring for Your Teeth as You Age

Oral Health & Overall Wellness5 min read

Our smiles change throughout our lives, and the years after middle age bring a new set of considerations for keeping teeth and gums healthy. Good news: with consistent habits and regular professional care, many people keep their natural teeth for a lifetime. Understanding what changes with age helps you adapt your routine and stay ahead of common concerns.

What Changes Over Time

As we age, tooth enamel naturally wears, gums may recede slightly, and the nerves inside teeth can become less sensitive, meaning a cavity may progress without obvious discomfort. Years of wear also make older fillings and crowns more likely to need attention. Dry mouth, often a side effect of medications, becomes more common and can raise the risk of decay. Staying attentive to these shifts, and keeping up with a trusted dental care team, helps catch issues early when they are easier to manage.

Everyday Habits That Help

The fundamentals still matter, and small adjustments can make them easier if dexterity or vision becomes a challenge.

  • Brush twice daily with a soft-bristled or electric toothbrush and fluoride toothpaste.
  • Clean between teeth daily with floss, interdental brushes, or a water flosser.
  • Stay hydrated and ask about saliva substitutes if dry mouth is an issue.
  • Limit sugary snacks and sip water rather than sweetened drinks.
  • If you wear dentures, clean them daily and remove them overnight unless advised otherwise.

Protecting Gums and Existing Dental Work

Gum health is closely tied to overall wellness, so keeping inflammation in check supports both your mouth and your body. Mention any new medications, swallowing difficulties, or changes in how your bite feels, since these can affect your care plan. Routine checkups allow your dentist to monitor older restorations, screen for oral cancer, and adjust recommendations to fit your evolving needs.
