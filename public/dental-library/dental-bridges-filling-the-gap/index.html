Dental Bridges: Filling the Gap

Restorative Dentistry & Tooth Replacement5 min read

Losing a tooth can affect more than your appearance. A gap in your smile may make it harder to chew comfortably or speak clearly, and over time neighboring teeth can begin to shift. A dental bridge is a time-tested solution that literally bridges the space left by one or more missing teeth, restoring both the look and the function of your smile.

How a Bridge Works

A traditional dental bridge is made up of one or more artificial teeth, called pontics, that are anchored in place by crowns on the neighboring natural teeth or by dental implants. Once secured, the bridge fills the gap and distributes your bite more evenly. Bridges are custom-crafted to match the color and shape of your surrounding teeth, so the final result blends in naturally.

Reasons People Choose a Bridge

Patients often consider a bridge for several practical reasons:

  • Restoring the ability to chew a wider variety of foods
  • Supporting clearer speech affected by a missing tooth
  • Helping maintain the natural shape of the face
  • Keeping adjacent teeth from drifting into the empty space
  • Renewing confidence in a complete-looking smile

Whether a bridge is the right fit depends on the health of your remaining teeth and gums. The team at our dental practice can help you compare a bridge with other options, such as implants, so you can make an informed decision.

Caring for Your Bridge

With good care, a dental bridge can serve you well for many years. Daily brushing and cleaning beneath the bridge, often with floss threaders or small interdental brushes, help keep the supporting teeth and gums healthy. Regular dental visits allow your team to check the fit and condition of the bridge over time. A balanced diet and avoiding habits like chewing ice also help protect your restoration.
