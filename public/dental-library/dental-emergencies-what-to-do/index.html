Dental Emergencies: What to Do

Emergency & Urgent Care6 min read

Dental emergencies can happen when you least expect them, whether from a sports injury, a fall, or sudden severe pain. Knowing how to respond in those first few minutes can ease your discomfort and, in some cases, help save a tooth. While this guide offers general first-aid steps, it is important to contact a dental professional promptly for any urgent problem.

Common Dental Emergencies and First Steps

Different situations call for slightly different responses. Here are some common emergencies and what you can do right away:

  • Knocked-out tooth: Pick it up by the crown, not the root, gently rinse if dirty, and try to place it back in the socket. If that is not possible, keep it in milk or saliva and seek care immediately.
  • Severe toothache: Rinse with warm water, gently floss to remove any trapped debris, and use a cold compress on the outside of your cheek to reduce swelling.
  • Cracked or broken tooth: Rinse your mouth, save any pieces, and apply a cold compress to limit swelling until you can be seen.
  • Lost filling or crown: Keep the area clean and avoid chewing on that side until it can be repaired.
  • Soft tissue injury: Apply gentle pressure with clean gauze to control bleeding, and use a cold compress for comfort.

When to Seek Immediate Help

Some signs mean you should not wait. Significant swelling, bleeding that does not stop, a high fever alongside dental pain, or difficulty breathing or swallowing may point to a serious infection or injury that needs prompt attention. If you are unsure how urgent your situation is, it is always wise to call a dental office for guidance. You can contact our dental office to describe your symptoms and get advice on the next steps.

Preventing Emergencies When You Can

While not every emergency is avoidable, a few habits lower your risk. Wearing a mouthguard during sports, avoiding chewing on ice or hard objects, and keeping up with regular check-ups all help catch small problems before they become painful ones. Being prepared with a plan and your dentist's number saved can also reduce stress if something does happen.
