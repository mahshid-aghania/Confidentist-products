Dental Fillings: Types and Materials

Restorative Dentistry & Tooth Replacement5 min read

Dental fillings are one of the most common ways to treat a cavity and restore a tooth to healthy function. When decay removes part of your tooth structure, a filling replaces that space, seals out bacteria, and helps you chew and speak comfortably again. Today there are several filling materials to choose from, and the right option depends on the location of the tooth, the size of the cavity, your appearance goals, and your budget. Understanding the choices can help you feel more confident during your next visit.

Common Filling Materials

Each material has its own strengths, and your dentist can help you weigh them based on your specific situation.

  • Composite resin: A tooth-coloured material that blends naturally with your smile, making it popular for front teeth and visible areas.
  • Amalgam: A durable silver-coloured mix of metals that has been used reliably for generations, often chosen for back teeth that handle heavy chewing.
  • Glass ionomer: A material that releases fluoride over time and is frequently used for small fillings or near the gum line.
  • Gold and ceramic: Longer-lasting options that are custom-made in a lab and known for their strength and stability.

How Your Dentist Chooses

There is no single filling that is ideal for every tooth. A small cavity in a front tooth may call for composite resin so the repair stays discreet, while a large cavity in a molar might benefit from a stronger material. During your exam at the team at Smile Dental Arts Centre, your dentist will consider how much healthy tooth remains, how the tooth meets its neighbours when you bite, and your personal preferences before recommending an approach.

Caring for Your Filling

A well-placed filling can last many years with good habits. Brush twice daily, floss once a day, and keep up with regular checkups so your dentist can spot any wear early. It is normal for a tooth to feel slightly sensitive for a few days after a filling, but let your dental team know if discomfort lingers or if your bite feels uneven, as a quick adjustment is usually all that is needed.

If you think you may have a cavity or want to review your options, schedule a consultation with Smile Dental Arts Centre to find the filling that fits your smile.
