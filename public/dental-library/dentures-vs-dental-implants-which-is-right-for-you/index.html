Dentures vs. Dental Implants: Which Is Right for You?

Restorative Dentistry & Tooth Replacement5 min read

Losing one or more teeth can affect your confidence, your diet, and the way you speak. The good news is that today there are reliable ways to replace missing teeth, with dentures and dental implants being two of the most common. Each option has its own advantages, and the best choice depends on your oral health, your budget, and your personal preferences. Understanding how they compare can help you have a more informed conversation with your dentist.

Understanding Dentures

Dentures are removable appliances that replace missing teeth and the surrounding tissue. They can be complete, replacing a full arch of teeth, or partial, filling in gaps while resting against your remaining natural teeth. Dentures are often a more affordable and non-surgical way to restore a smile, and they can usually be fitted relatively quickly. They do require daily removal and cleaning, and they may need occasional adjustments as your mouth changes over time.

Understanding Dental Implants

A dental implant is a small titanium post placed in the jawbone to act as an artificial tooth root, topped with a custom crown. Implants are fixed in place, so they look and feel much like natural teeth and do not need to be removed. Because they integrate with the bone, they can also help preserve jaw structure over time. Placing an implant involves a surgical procedure and a healing period, so good general health and adequate bone are important considerations. The clinicians at Smile Dental Arts Centre can evaluate whether you are a suitable candidate.

Comparing Your Options

  • Stability: Implants stay fixed, while dentures are removable and may shift
  • Cost: Dentures typically have a lower upfront cost than implants
  • Procedure: Dentures are non-surgical; implants require surgery and healing time
  • Bone health: Implants can help maintain jawbone, whereas dentures do not
  • Maintenance: Dentures need daily removal; implants are cared for like natural teeth

Making the Decision

There is no single answer that fits everyone. Some patients prefer the convenience and feel of implants, while others find that dentures meet their needs comfortably and affordably. In some cases, a combination approach, such as implant-supported dentures, offers the benefits of both. A thorough examination, including imaging, is the best way to understand which path suits you.
