Dry Mouth: Causes and Solutions

Common Concerns & Symptoms5 min read

That sticky, parched feeling known as dry mouth, or xerostomia, is more than a minor annoyance. When your mouth does not produce enough saliva, it can affect your comfort, your ability to eat and speak, and the long-term health of your teeth. The good news is that most cases can be managed well once the cause is understood.

Common Causes

Dry mouth happens when the salivary glands slow down, and many everyday factors can contribute.

  • Medications, including those for allergies, blood pressure, anxiety, and depression.
  • Dehydration from illness, heat, or simply not drinking enough fluids.
  • Mouth breathing, often during sleep or because of nasal congestion.
  • Certain medical conditions and treatments, such as some therapies for other health issues.
  • Tobacco, alcohol, and high caffeine intake.

Why It Matters

Because saliva protects teeth by washing away bacteria and neutralizing acids, a lasting lack of it can raise the risk of cavities, gum irritation, and bad breath. Some people also notice difficulty swallowing, a sore throat, or a changed sense of taste. If dryness persists, sharing the details with your trusted dentist helps identify the cause and the right approach for you.

Practical Solutions

Relief often comes from a combination of simple habits. Sip water throughout the day, chew sugar-free gum to stimulate saliva, and use a humidifier at night. Over-the-counter saliva substitutes and moisturizing rinses can help, and your dentist may recommend fluoride treatments to guard against decay. If a medication seems to be the culprit, your physician may be able to adjust it. Staying attentive and keeping up with regular visits makes dry mouth far more manageable.
