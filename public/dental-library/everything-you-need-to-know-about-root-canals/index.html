Everything You Need to Know About Root Canals

Restorative Dentistry & Tooth Replacement6 min read

Few dental procedures have as many misconceptions attached to them as the root canal. In reality, it is a routine treatment designed to relieve pain and save a tooth that might otherwise need to be removed. Understanding what the procedure involves can ease the worry many people feel when they first hear it recommended.

When a Root Canal Is Needed

Inside each tooth is a soft tissue called the pulp, which contains nerves and blood vessels. When the pulp becomes infected or inflamed, treatment is often necessary to prevent further problems. Common signs that may lead your dentist to suggest a root canal include:

  • Persistent toothache, especially when chewing or applying pressure
  • Lingering sensitivity to hot or cold temperatures
  • Swelling or tenderness in the nearby gums
  • Darkening of the tooth or a deep cavity reaching the pulp

What Happens During Treatment

A root canal removes the affected pulp, cleans the inside of the tooth, and seals it to prevent reinfection. The area is numbed beforehand, so the experience is often compared to having a filling placed. After the pulp is removed and the space is cleaned, the tooth is filled and sealed. In many cases, a crown is later added to protect the tooth and restore its strength. If you have questions about the process, the professionals at Smile Dental Arts Centre can walk you through what to expect.

Recovery and Aftercare

Most people return to their normal routine shortly after the procedure. Mild tenderness for a few days is common and usually manageable with the care instructions your dentist provides. Keeping the treated tooth clean and attending follow-up visits helps support healing and the long-term health of the restoration.

Saving a natural tooth through a root canal can help you maintain normal biting and chewing while avoiding the gap left by an extraction. Prompt attention to tooth pain often makes treatment more straightforward.
