Gingivitis vs. Periodontitis: Knowing the Difference

Gum & Periodontal Health5 min read

Gum health often receives less attention than the teeth themselves, yet the tissues supporting your teeth are essential to a healthy mouth. Two terms you may hear are gingivitis and periodontitis. Though related, they describe different stages of gum disease, and understanding the distinction can help you recognize when to act.

What Is Gingivitis?

Gingivitis is inflammation of the gums, usually caused by plaque that accumulates along the gumline. Its signs are often subtle, which is why it can go unnoticed. Common indicators include redness, puffiness, and gums that bleed during brushing or flossing. Importantly, gingivitis affects only the gum tissue and does not yet involve the bone that supports the teeth. With improved daily care and professional cleaning, gingivitis can often be reversed.

  • Red or swollen gums
  • Bleeding when brushing or flossing
  • Tenderness along the gumline
  • Persistent bad breath

How Periodontitis Differs

When gum inflammation goes unaddressed, it can progress to periodontitis. At this stage, the inflammation extends beyond the gums to the structures that hold teeth in place, including the bone. Gums may begin to pull away from the teeth, forming pockets where bacteria collect. Over time, this can lead to bone loss and loosening of the teeth. Unlike gingivitis, the damage from periodontitis is generally not fully reversible, though ongoing care can help manage it and slow its progression. If you notice receding gums or loosening teeth, the periodontal team at our practice can assess your gum health.

Protecting Your Gums

The encouraging news is that gum disease is largely preventable with consistent habits. Brushing twice daily, cleaning between the teeth, and attending regular dental visits allow problems to be caught early, when they are easiest to address. Professional cleanings remove hardened deposits that brushing alone cannot, and routine check-ups help monitor the health of your gums over time.
