Mouthguards: Protecting Your Smile During Sports

Preventive Care & Checkups5 min read

Sports and physical activity offer wonderful benefits, but they also carry a risk of dental injury. A single impact can chip, loosen, or knock out a tooth, leading to discomfort and treatment that could often have been avoided. A mouthguard is a simple, comfortable way to help shield your teeth and soft tissues while you stay active.

Why Mouthguards Matter

A mouthguard cushions blows to the face, helping to distribute the force of an impact and reduce the chance of injury to teeth, lips, and the jaw. Dental injuries are among the more common sports-related injuries, and they are not limited to contact sports. Activities such as cycling, skateboarding, and gymnastics also carry a risk of falls and collisions.

  • Helps protect teeth from chips, fractures, and being knocked out
  • Cushions the lips and cheeks against the teeth
  • May help reduce the impact transmitted to the jaw
  • Suitable for both competitive and recreational athletes

Types of Mouthguards

Mouthguards come in a few forms, each with trade-offs in fit and convenience. Stock guards are ready to wear but often bulky and loose. Boil-and-bite versions soften in hot water and mold somewhat to your teeth, offering a closer fit. Custom-made guards, created by a dentist from an impression of your mouth, tend to provide the most comfortable and secure fit, which can make them easier to wear consistently. To explore a custom option, you can speak with the team at our clinic about what might suit your activity.

Caring for Your Mouthguard

A well-maintained guard lasts longer and stays hygienic. Rinse it after each use, clean it gently with a brush, and store it in a ventilated case away from heat. Bring it to dental visits so its fit and condition can be checked, and replace it if it becomes torn or no longer fits well, which is especially common for growing children and teens.
