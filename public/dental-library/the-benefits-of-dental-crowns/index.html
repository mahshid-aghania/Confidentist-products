The Benefits of Dental Crowns

Restorative Dentistry & Tooth Replacement4 min read

A dental crown is a custom-made cap that fits over a tooth to restore its shape, strength, and appearance. When a tooth has been weakened by decay, a large filling, a crack, or root canal treatment, a crown can help protect what remains of the natural tooth and keep it working comfortably for years. Crowns are one of the most common and dependable restorations in modern dentistry, and understanding how they help can make the idea of treatment feel much less intimidating.

Why Your Dentist May Recommend a Crown

Crowns do more than cover a tooth cosmetically. They distribute the force of chewing, hold cracked teeth together, and shield a tooth that no longer has enough healthy structure to support a simple filling. If you have ever been told that a tooth is "too far gone for a filling," a crown is often the restoration that allows you to keep the tooth rather than lose it. The team at Smile Dental Arts Centre can assess whether a crown is the most suitable option for your situation.

Key Benefits

  • Protects and strengthens a tooth that is cracked, worn, or heavily filled
  • Restores comfortable biting and chewing function
  • Covers and supports a tooth after root canal treatment
  • Improves the shape, size, and colour of a damaged or discoloured tooth
  • Can be matched to blend naturally with surrounding teeth

What Materials Are Available

Modern crowns can be made from porcelain, ceramic, metal, or a combination of materials. Tooth-coloured ceramics are popular for front teeth because they mimic the translucency of natural enamel, while stronger materials may be chosen for back teeth that handle heavier chewing. Your dentist will discuss which option best balances durability and appearance for your specific tooth.

Caring for Your Crown

A crown does not get cavities the way natural enamel does, but the tooth underneath and the gum around it still need daily attention. Brushing twice a day, cleaning between your teeth, and attending regular check-ups all help a crown last as long as possible. Avoiding habits like chewing ice or using your teeth as tools can also protect your investment.
