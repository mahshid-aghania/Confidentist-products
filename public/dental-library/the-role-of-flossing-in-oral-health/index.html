The Role of Flossing in Oral Health

Oral Hygiene & Daily Care4 min read

Brushing cleans the surfaces of your teeth, but it cannot reach everywhere. The tight spaces between your teeth and along the gumline are where plaque often lingers, and that is exactly where flossing helps. Adding this simple step to your daily routine can make a real difference in keeping your gums healthy and your breath fresh.

Why Flossing Matters

Food particles and plaque can collect between teeth throughout the day. When left in place, they may contribute to tooth decay and irritation of the gums. Flossing gently removes this buildup from spots a toothbrush simply cannot access, supporting the health of the areas you cannot easily see or reach.

The professionals at Smile Dental Arts Centre often remind patients that flossing is a helpful companion to brushing, not a replacement, each step plays its own important role.

How to Floss Comfortably

  • Use about 18 inches of floss, winding most around your middle fingers
  • Guide the floss gently between your teeth, avoiding a hard snap against the gums
  • Curve the floss into a C-shape around each tooth
  • Move it gently up and down along the side of each tooth
  • Use a fresh section of floss as you move from tooth to tooth

Finding What Works for You

If traditional floss feels awkward, there are other options to explore, including floss picks, water flossers, and interdental brushes. The right choice is the one you will use consistently. A little mild tenderness is common when you first begin, but it usually eases as your gums grow healthier. If bleeding continues, mention it to your dentist so they can take a closer look.
