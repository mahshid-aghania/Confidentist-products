Understanding Dental X-Rays

Preventive Care & Checkups4 min read

Dental X-rays, also known as radiographs, are an important diagnostic tool that lets your dentist see what is happening beneath the surface of your teeth and gums. Many dental concerns begin in places that cannot be seen during a visual exam, and X-rays help reveal them early. Understanding how these images work, and why they are taken, can make your next visit feel more reassuring.

What X-Rays Can Reveal

A quick set of images can show a surprising amount of useful detail. Dental X-rays help your dentist identify:

  • Cavities forming between teeth or under existing fillings
  • Bone levels and early signs of gum disease
  • Impacted teeth, including wisdom teeth below the gumline
  • Infections or cysts at the root of a tooth
  • The position of teeth to guide orthodontic or implant planning

By spotting these issues early, your dentist can often recommend simpler, more conservative treatment. The team at Smile Dental Arts Centre will explain any findings in plain language so you understand what the images show.

Are Dental X-Rays Safe?

Modern dental X-rays use very low levels of radiation, and digital imaging has reduced exposure even further compared with older film methods. Your dental team also takes protective steps, such as using lead aprons or thyroid collars when appropriate and taking only the images that are needed. If you are pregnant or think you may be, let your dentist know so your care can be adjusted accordingly.

How Often Are They Needed

There is no one-size-fits-all schedule. How often you need X-rays depends on your age, oral health history, and risk of dental problems. Someone with a history of cavities may need images more frequently, while a patient with stable oral health may need them less often. Your dentist will tailor the timing to your individual situation rather than following a fixed routine.
