Understanding Tooth Decay and Cavities

Preventive Care & Checkups5 min read

Tooth decay is one of the most common dental concerns, affecting people of all ages. It happens gradually, which is why understanding how it develops can help you take steps to prevent it. With good daily habits and regular care, cavities are often very manageable, and many can be avoided altogether.

What Causes Tooth Decay?

Your mouth is home to natural bacteria that feed on sugars and starches from the foods you eat. As they do, they produce acids that can gradually wear away tooth enamel. Over time, this process may create a small hole, known as a cavity. When caught early, decay can often be addressed before it reaches the deeper, more sensitive layers of the tooth.

Signs to Watch For

  • Sensitivity to hot, cold, or sweet foods and drinks
  • A toothache or discomfort when biting down
  • Visible pits or small holes in a tooth
  • White, brown, or dark spots on the tooth surface

Keep in mind that early decay often causes no symptoms at all, which is one reason regular checkups are so valuable. During an exam, the team at Smile Dental Arts Centre can spot changes long before you would notice them yourself.

Prevention and Treatment

Preventing decay starts with the basics: brushing twice a day with fluoride toothpaste, flossing daily, and limiting frequent sugary snacks and drinks. Drinking water and maintaining regular dental visits also help protect your enamel. When a cavity does form, treatment is usually straightforward and may involve a filling to restore the tooth. Addressing decay promptly helps keep your tooth strong and comfortable for the long run.
