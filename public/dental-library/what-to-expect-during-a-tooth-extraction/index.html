What to Expect During a Tooth Extraction

Oral Surgery6 min read

Hearing that you need a tooth removed can feel unsettling, but a tooth extraction is a common and well-understood procedure. Dentists recommend it only when it is the best way to protect your overall oral health, such as with severe decay, advanced gum disease, crowding, or a tooth that cannot be repaired. Knowing what happens before, during, and after can help you feel calm and prepared.

Before the Procedure

Your dentist will review your medical history, take any needed X-rays, and explain your options. This is the ideal time to ask questions and share concerns about anxiety or past experiences. You may receive instructions about eating, medications, or arranging a ride home if sedation is planned. A thorough consultation at Smile Dental Arts Centre ensures the plan is tailored to your needs.

During the Extraction

The area around the tooth is numbed with local anesthetic so you stay comfortable. You may feel gentle pressure or movement, but you should not feel sharp pain. For a simple extraction, the tooth is loosened and lifted out. More complex cases, such as an impacted tooth, may involve a small incision. The process is usually quicker than patients expect.

Caring for Yourself Afterward

Good aftercare supports comfortable healing. Your dentist will give you detailed instructions, which often include:

  • Biting gently on gauze to help a blood clot form.
  • Resting and avoiding strenuous activity for the first day.
  • Eating soft, cool foods and staying hydrated.
  • Avoiding straws, smoking, and vigorous rinsing early on.
  • Taking any recommended pain relief as directed.

When to Call Your Dentist

Mild swelling and tenderness are normal and usually ease within a few days. Reach out to your dental team if you notice increasing pain, heavy bleeding, fever, or signs that the clot has dislodged. Prompt communication helps you stay on the smoothest path to recovery.
