Wisdom Teeth: When Removal Is Recommended

Oral Surgery5 min read

Wisdom teeth are the last molars to emerge, usually appearing in the late teens or early twenties. For some people they come in without trouble, while for others they cause discomfort or complications. Knowing when removal may be recommended can help you understand your dentist's advice.

Why Wisdom Teeth Can Be a Problem

Because they arrive last, wisdom teeth often have limited room to emerge properly. When space is tight, they may become impacted, meaning they are unable to fully break through the gum. This can lead to a range of concerns that your dentist will monitor over time.

  • Crowding or pressure on neighboring teeth
  • Partial eruption that makes cleaning difficult and invites decay
  • Gum inflammation or recurring infection around the tooth
  • Cysts or damage to nearby teeth in some cases

When Removal May Be Advised

Not every wisdom tooth needs to come out. If they are healthy, fully erupted, properly positioned, and easy to clean, your dentist may recommend simply keeping an eye on them. Removal is more likely to be suggested when there is pain, infection, decay, or a risk of future problems based on their position. Dental images help your dentist assess how the teeth are developing. The clinical team at Smile Dental Arts Centre can evaluate your wisdom teeth and explain whether monitoring or removal makes more sense for you.

What to Expect With Removal

Wisdom tooth removal is a common procedure performed with appropriate anesthesia for comfort. Recovery varies, but most people manage well by resting, eating soft foods, and following the aftercare guidance they receive. Some swelling or tenderness in the first few days is normal and tends to ease with time.

Regular checkups are the best way to track how your wisdom teeth are developing, so any issues can be addressed before they become more complicated.
