The NDEB Equivalency Process & Exams

The Assessment of Fundamental Knowledge (AFK) is the first written exam of the NDEB Equivalency Process. It is a multiple-choice exam covering the biomedical and dental sciences, and candidates typically take it first on their path to Canadian licensure. Learn more about our AFK preparation.

The Assessment of Clinical Judgement (ACJ) is the second written exam of the NDEB Equivalency Process. It uses clinical scenarios to test your diagnosis, treatment planning and clinical decision-making. See our ACJ preparation.

The NDECC is the clinical examination of the NDEB Equivalency Process. It assesses your clinical skills and situational judgement through hands-on and scenario-based stations, and is typically the final equivalency assessment after the AFK and ACJ. See our NDECC preparation.

The Advanced Dental Admission Test (ADAT) is a standardized exam administered by the American Dental Association. Some dental schools use ADAT scores to evaluate applicants — often for advanced-standing / degree-qualifying programs. It covers biomedical sciences, clinical sciences, and data, research interpretation and evidence-based dentistry.

The equivalency exams are generally taken in sequence — the AFK first, then the ACJ, and finally the NDECC. Confirm the current prerequisites and ordering on the NDEB website, as eligibility rules can change.

The NDEB sets a maximum number of attempts for each exam. Because these limits are updated from time to time, check the current attempt limits directly on the NDEB website before you plan your exam timeline.

Each exam runs on its own schedule, typically a few sittings per year with set registration windows. See the NDEB's official examination calendar for current dates, and plan your preparation around the sitting you intend to take.

Availability of international test locations for the written exams has changed over time, and the clinical NDECC is administered in Canada. Always confirm the current testing locations and options on the NDEB website before registering.

There is no fixed timeline — it depends on how quickly you prepare for and pass each exam, and on exam scheduling. Many candidates complete the three assessments over roughly one to three years. Structured preparation and mock exams can help you pass on fewer attempts and move through the process faster.

University Programs, Visas & Licensure

The NDEB Equivalency Process is an examination route (AFK, ACJ, NDECC) that leads to NDEB certification. A degree qualifying / advanced-standing program is a 2–3 year university program for internationally trained dentists that awards a Canadian DDS/DMD. Both can lead to eligibility for licensure; which fits you depends on your goals, budget and timeline.

You apply directly through each university's admissions process for its qualifying / advanced-standing program. Requirements vary by school and commonly include AFK results, ADAT scores, English-language test results, a bench/skills test and an interview. Review each program's requirements carefully, and consider our university interview preparation.

To study in a Canadian university program you will generally need a valid study permit (student visa), issued by Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC). Requirements and processing times change, so confirm the current rules with IRCC or the university's international student office.

If you only need to enter Canada to sit an exam, valid visitor status (a visitor visa or eTA, depending on your nationality) is often sufficient — a study permit is not necessarily required just to write an exam. Immigration requirements vary by situation and change over time, so confirm what you need with IRCC and check the NDEB's guidance for exam candidates.

Completing the Equivalency Process (or an accredited qualifying program) makes you eligible to apply for licensure, but licences are granted by each province's or territory's dental regulatory authority, which usually has additional requirements — for example a jurisprudence exam, registration, and background checks. Check the specific requirements of the regulator where you intend to practice.

International dentists obtain NDEB certification by either completing the NDEB Equivalency Process (AFK, ACJ and NDECC) or graduating from an accredited dental program (including a Canadian qualifying/advanced-standing program), and then meeting the NDEB's certification requirements. The NDEB issues the certificate; provincial licensure is a separate, additional step.

The typical path is: 1) establish equivalency (through the Equivalency Process or an accredited program); 2) obtain NDEB certification; and 3) apply to the provincial or territorial dental regulator for a licence, meeting their additional requirements (such as a jurisprudence exam and registration). See our preparation roadmap for an overview of the journey.

Universities and regulators commonly accept recognized tests such as IELTS (Academic) or TOEFL, with minimum scores that vary by institution. Always check the exact test and score required by the specific university program or regulator you are applying to.
